\section{Introduction}
% 第一段：PDP/data integrity的重要性
% 第二段：多项式承诺广泛应用在data integrity中
% 第三段：用户的文件多，逐个验证代价大
% 第四段：由于文件大，data integrity通常通过抽样检查
% 第五段：抽样过程中如果抽样点、掩码不合理，会导致隐私泄露

The exponential growth of global data generation has driven a paradigm shift toward decentralized and cloud-based storage architectures. By outsourcing data to remote storage providers, individuals and organizations significantly reduce local hardware maintenance costs while benefiting from elastic scalability, geographic redundancy, and high availability. However, this outsourcing model inherently relinquishes physical control over the stored assets, exposing them to multifaceted risks such as hardware degradation, silent data corruption, and economically motivated data deletion or compression by untrusted storage providers. To mitigate these threats and restore trust in distributed storage ecosystems, Provable Data Possession (PDP) and Proof of Retrievability (PoR) schemes have emerged as foundational cryptographic primitives. These protocols enable data owners or delegated auditors to efficiently verify the integrity of outsourced data without retrieving the entire dataset, thereby ensuring continuous data availability, regulatory compliance, and accountability in modern storage networks.

Recent advancements in cryptographic primitives have significantly optimized the efficiency and scalability of data integrity auditing. Among these, Polynomial Commitment Schemes (PCS), particularly the Kate-Zaverucha-Goldberg (KZG) construction, have been widely adopted in state-of-the-art PDP designs. By encoding data blocks as coefficients of low-degree polynomials over a finite field, PCS enables compact, constant-size commitments and supports homomorphic aggregation of multiple block tags. This algebraic structure allows cloud servers to generate succinct proofs of possession through polynomial evaluation and quotient computation, while verifiers can validate correctness using a minimal number of bilinear pairings. Consequently, polynomial commitments have become the cornerstone of lightweight, publicly verifiable auditing frameworks, drastically reducing communication bandwidth and on-chain storage overhead compared to traditional hash-based or signature-based approaches. Their compatibility with structured reference strings (SRS) and efficient synthetic division algorithms further positions them as ideal candidates for large-scale storage verification.

Despite these cryptographic improvements, practical storage deployments frequently involve massive-scale data repositories comprising thousands of files distributed across multiple storage nodes, providers, or geographic regions. Conventional PDP protocols process each file independently, requiring separate proof generation, transmission, and verification rounds for every audited dataset. This linear scaling introduces severe computational and economic bottlenecks, particularly in blockchain-assisted auditing systems where smart contract execution costs (gas) and on-chain proof storage scale directly with the number of files. As audit frequencies increase and batch sizes grow, the cumulative overhead of independent verifications rapidly degrades system throughput, inflates operational expenses, and strains verifier resources. Therefore, developing a secure and efficient multi-file batch verification mechanism that compresses multiple integrity checks into a single, constant-size cryptographic operation has become a critical research imperative for scalable decentralized storage.

Given the substantial size of individual files in real-world applications, auditing every data block is computationally prohibitive and economically unviable. To address this challenge, modern PDP schemes universally employ probabilistic sampling strategies. During an audit round, the verifier randomly selects a subset of data blocks and assigns independent random coefficients to construct a linear combination of challenged sectors. This approach reduces the proof size and verification complexity from linear $O(n)$ to constant or logarithmic $O(c)$ with respect to the total number of blocks, where $c \ll n$ denotes the challenge size. By carefully calibrating the number of sampled blocks, auditors can achieve arbitrarily high detection probabilities for data corruption while maintaining minimal communication and computation overhead. The randomness of the challenge set ensures that malicious providers cannot precompute proofs or selectively store only frequently accessed blocks without facing a high risk of detection.

However, the introduction of probabilistic sampling and homomorphic aggregation introduces subtle yet critical privacy vulnerabilities. In many existing batch auditing designs, challenge generation mechanisms, evaluation points, and masking techniques are either oversimplified or structurally coupled across files. If challenge points are shared, folding weights are predictable, or masking scalars lack statistically hiding commitments, an honest-but-curious verifier or public ledger observer can exploit the linear algebraic structure of aggregated evaluations. Through cross-round correlation analysis, low-entropy candidate testing, or linear system inversion on publicly disclosed challenge-response transcripts, adversaries may reconstruct underlying polynomial evaluations $f(r_i)$ or even infer sensitive sector values. Such privacy leakage fundamentally contradicts the confidentiality requirements of modern cloud and decentralized storage, highlighting the necessity of integrating rigorous one-time masking with hiding commitments directly into the batch verification algebraic structure to prevent statistical inference attacks.

To address the aforementioned challenges, we propose \textbf{FoldAudit}, a novel batch-verifiable PDP scheme that simultaneously achieves efficient multi-file verification, tight cryptographic soundness, and robust evaluation privacy. FoldAudit introduces a layered algebraic folding architecture: (i) independent per-file challenge points $\{r_i\}$ to structurally isolate file-specific quotient polynomials and prevent cross-file pole cancellation; (ii) a Fiat-Shamir derived random folding scalar $\rho$ applied after the proof statement is fixed, ensuring that batch aggregation remains algebraically unpredictable and bias-resistant; and (iii) statistically hiding Pedersen mask commitments verified through zero-knowledge masked-opening Sigma proofs. The folded KZG relation yields a soundness error bounded by $\epsilon \leq \frac{m-1}{p}+\mathsf{negl}(\lambda)$ for challenged corrupted data, guaranteeing that adversarial cross-file cancellation is detected with overwhelming probability. Theoretical analysis and experimental evaluations demonstrate that FoldAudit keeps verification dominated by challenged-tag aggregation and a concrete masked-opening Sigma proof, supports stateless dynamic file updates without global state recomputation, and maintains provable privacy against honest-but-curious auditors, making it highly suitable for large-scale, privacy-sensitive decentralized storage environments.

\subsection{Basics of Polynomials}

Let $\FF_p$ be a finite field of prime order $p$, where $p$ is super-polynomial in the security parameter $\lambda$. For an integer $d>0$, let $\FF_p^{<d}[X]$ denote the set of univariate polynomials over $\FF_p$ of degree strictly smaller than $d$. We write $[m]=\{1,\ldots,m\}$ and use uppercase letters for indeterminates (e.g., $X$) and lowercase letters for field elements (e.g., $x,r,z\in\FF_p$). All polynomial identities in this paper are over $\FF_p[X]$, and all exponents of group elements are interpreted modulo $p$.

\paragraph{Quotient Polynomials.}
For $f(X)\in\FF_p[X]$ and $r,y\in\FF_p$, the polynomial remainder theorem implies
\[
    f(X)-y=(X-r)w(X)+(f(r)-y)
\]
for a unique quotient polynomial $w(X)\in\FF_p[X]$. Hence $(X-r)$ divides $f(X)-y$ if and only if $y=f(r)$, in which case
\[
    w(X)=\frac{f(X)-y}{X-r}
\]
is well-defined and satisfies $\deg(w)\le \deg(f)-1$. FoldAudit uses this divisibility relation as the algebraic statement certified by each per-file KZG witness.

\begin{lemma}[Schwartz--Zippel]\label{lem:schwartz_zippel}
Let $P(X)\in\FF_p[X]$ be a non-zero polynomial of degree at most $d$. For uniformly sampled $\rho\leftarrow\FF_p$,
\[
    \Pr[P(\rho)=0]\le \frac{d}{p}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Random Linear Folding]\label{lem:random_linear_folding}
Let $\Delta_1,\ldots,\Delta_m\in\FF_p$ be field elements that are fixed before $\rho$ is sampled. If at least one $\Delta_i$ is non-zero and $\rho\leftarrow\FF_p$ is uniform, then
\[
    \Pr_\rho\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^m \rho^{i-1}\Delta_i=0\right]\le \frac{m-1}{p}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The folded error polynomial $P_\Delta(T)=\sum_{i=1}^m \Delta_iT^{i-1}$ is non-zero and has degree at most $m-1$. The claim follows immediately from Lemma~\ref{lem:schwartz_zippel}.
\end{proof}



\subsection{KZG Polynomial Commitment}
We use the Kate--Zaverucha--Goldberg (KZG) polynomial commitment scheme~\cite{Kate2010KZG} over bilinear groups $(\GG_1,\GG_2,\GG_T,e)$ of prime order $p$. The pairing $e:\GG_1\times\GG_2\rightarrow\GG_T$ is efficiently computable, bilinear, and non-degenerate. Let $g\in\GG_1$ and $h\in\GG_2$ be generators.

\paragraph{Setup.}
A setup algorithm samples a trapdoor $\tau\leftarrow\FF_p$ and publishes the KZG part of the SRS supporting degree-$<s$ polynomials:
\[
    \SRS=\left(g,g^\tau,\ldots,g^{\tau^{s-1}},h,h^\tau\right).
\]
The trapdoor $\tau$ must remain hidden from any prover after setup.

\paragraph{Commitment.}
For $f(X)=\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}a_kX^k\in\FF_p^{<s}[X]$, the KZG commitment is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kzg_commitment}
    C_f := g^{f(\tau)}
    =\prod_{k=0}^{s-1}\left(g^{\tau^k}\right)^{a_k}\in\GG_1 .
\end{equation}
The commitment is additively homomorphic in the exponent: for commitments $C_f$ and $C_q$ and scalars $\alpha,\beta\in\FF_p$, one has
$
C_f^\alpha C_q^\beta=g^{(\alpha f+\beta q)(\tau)}.
$

\paragraph{Opening and Verification.}
To open $C_f$ at point $r\in\FF_p$ with value $y=f(r)$, the prover computes
\[
    w(X)=\frac{f(X)-y}{X-r},\qquad \pi=g^{w(\tau)} .
\]
The verifier accepts if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kzg_verify}
    e(C_f g^{-y},h)
    \stackrel{?}{=}
    e(\pi,h^\tau h^{-r}).
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:kzg_verify} is equivalent, in the exponent, to
$
f(\tau)-y=(\tau-r)w(\tau).
$
Under the standard $q$-SDH assumption for the SRS degree bound, KZG commitments are computationally binding: a PPT adversary cannot open the same commitment at the same point to two distinct values, nor open it to a value inconsistent with the committed polynomial, except with negligible probability.


\subsection{Multi-Polynomial Batch Evaluation (MPBE)}
\label{sec:multi_poly_batch_evaluation}
Batch verification in FoldAudit is based on random linear folding of multiple KZG quotient equations. Suppose the verifier must check $m$ claims $f_i(r_i)=y_i$ with commitments $b_i=g^{f_i(\tau)}$ and quotient commitments $C_{w_i}=g^{w_i(\tau)}$. The individual KZG relation for file $i$ can be written as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:individual_kzg_relation}
    e\!\left(b_i g^{-y_i} C_{w_i}^{r_i},h\right)
    \stackrel{?}{=}
    e(C_{w_i},h^\tau).
\end{equation}
Indeed, if $w_i(X)=(f_i(X)-y_i)/(X-r_i)$, then
$
b_i g^{-y_i}C_{w_i}^{r_i}=g^{\tau w_i(\tau)}.
$

Verifying all equations in~\eqref{eq:individual_kzg_relation} separately would require $O(m)$ pairings. Instead, after all commitments are fixed, the verifier samples a Fiat--Shamir folding challenge $\rho\in\FF_p$ and checks a single folded equation:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:folded_kzg_relation}
    e\!\left(
    \prod_{i=1}^m
    \left(b_i g^{-y_i} C_{w_i}^{r_i}\right)^{\rho^{i-1}},
    h\right)
    \stackrel{?}{=}
    e\!\left(
    \prod_{i=1}^m C_{w_i}^{\rho^{i-1}},
    h^\tau\right).
\end{equation}
If any individual equation has a non-zero exponent defect $\Delta_i$, then the folded equation can still hold only when $\sum_i\rho^{i-1}\Delta_i=0$, which occurs with probability at most $(m-1)/p$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:random_linear_folding}. This is the algebraic source of the batch soundness bound used in Theorem~\ref{thm:soundness}.

FoldAudit masks each value $y_i$ as $\tilde y_i=y_i+\mu_i$, but it does not publish a checkable single-base commitment $g^{\mu_i}$. Publishing such a value would let a verifier enumerate low-entropy candidates $y'$ and test whether $g^{\tilde y_i-y'}=g^{\mu_i}$. Instead, FoldAudit commits to the mask with a statistically hiding Pedersen commitment and verifies the folded relation in zero knowledge.

\subsection{Pedersen Mask Commitments}
Let $u\in\GG_1$ be an independent generator whose discrete logarithm with respect to $g$ is unknown. We use $u$ as the Pedersen blinding base because $h$ is already reserved for the $\GG_2$ pairing generator. For each file $i$, the prover samples $\theta_i\leftarrow\FF_p$ and publishes
\[
    R_i=g^{\mu_i}u^{\theta_i}.
\]
For any fixed $\mu_i$ and any commitment value $R_i$, the random blinding $\theta_i$ makes $R_i$ statistically independent of $\mu_i$. Consequently, the pair $(\tilde y_i,R_i)$ does not support the candidate test $R_i\stackrel{?}{=}g^{\tilde y_i-y'}$ that exists for the single-base commitment.

Since $R_i$ contains the extra factor $u^{\theta_i}$, it cannot be substituted directly into the public pairing equation. FoldAudit therefore uses a batched zero-knowledge proof $\Pi_{\mathsf{pm}}$ for the following masked-opening relation:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{R}_{\mathsf{pm}}=\{&
(\mathsf{stmt};\{\mu_i,\theta_i,\xi_i,w_i(X)\}_{i=1}^m),\ w_i\in\FF_p^{<s-1}[X]:\\
&R_i=g^{\mu_i}u^{\theta_i}\ \forall i,\quad
D_{w_i}=g^{w_i(\tau)}u^{\xi_i}\ \forall i,\\
&\text{and, for }C_{w_i}=g^{w_i(\tau)},\\
&e\!\left(
\prod_{i=1}^m
\left(b_i g^{\mu_i}g^{-\tilde y_i}C_{w_i}^{r_i}\right)^{\rho^{i-1}},h
\right)
=
e\!\left(
\prod_{i=1}^m C_{w_i}^{\rho^{i-1}},h^\tau
\right)\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The public statement $\mathsf{stmt}$ contains $(\mathsf{chal},\{\Gamma_i\},\{b_i\},\{\tilde y_i\},\{R_i\},\{D_{w_i}\},\rho)$ and the authenticated tags fixed by the verifier. The witnesses $\{\mu_i,\theta_i,\xi_i,w_i(X)\}$, and in particular the values $g^{\mu_i}$ and $C_{w_i}=g^{w_i(\tau)}$, are not revealed. The commitments $D_{w_i}$ are fixed before $\rho$ is derived, so the prover cannot choose quotient witnesses after seeing the folding scalar.

FoldAudit instantiates $\Pi_{\mathsf{pm}}$ with the following Fiat--Shamir Sigma protocol. Let $G_k=g^{\tau^k}$ for $0\le k\le s-2$, and write
$
w_i(X)=\sum_{k=0}^{s-2}d_{i,k}X^k.
$
The prover samples randomizers
$
\alpha_i,\beta_i,\gamma_i,\delta_{i,k}\leftarrow\FF_p
$
and computes
\[
    A_{R,i}=g^{\alpha_i}u^{\beta_i},
    \qquad
    A_{D,i}=\prod_{k=0}^{s-2}G_k^{\delta_{i,k}}u^{\gamma_i}.
\]
It also computes the folded relation commitment
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_1^0&=g^{\sum_i\rho^{i-1}\alpha_i}
      \prod_{k=0}^{s-2}G_k^{\sum_i\rho^{i-1}r_i\delta_{i,k}},\\
P_2^0&=\prod_{k=0}^{s-2}G_k^{\sum_i\rho^{i-1}\delta_{i,k}},\\
A_F&=e(P_1^0,h)\cdot e(P_2^0,h^\tau)^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The Fiat--Shamir challenge is
\[
    \chi=H_2(\mathsf{sigma\mbox{-}pm},\mathsf{stmt},\{A_{R,i},A_{D,i}\}_{i=1}^m,A_F).
\]
The responses are
\[
\begin{aligned}
z_{\mu,i}&=\alpha_i+\chi\mu_i,&
z_{\theta,i}&=\beta_i+\chi\theta_i,\\
z_{\xi,i}&=\gamma_i+\chi\xi_i,&
z_{i,k}&=\delta_{i,k}+\chi d_{i,k}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The proof is
\[
    \Pi_{\mathsf{pm}}=
    \left(A_F,\{A_{R,i},A_{D,i},z_{\mu,i},z_{\theta,i},z_{\xi,i},\{z_{i,k}\}_{k=0}^{s-2}\}_{i=1}^m\right).
\]
Verification recomputes $\chi$ and checks, for every $i$,
\[
    g^{z_{\mu,i}}u^{z_{\theta,i}}=A_{R,i}R_i^\chi,\qquad
    \prod_{k=0}^{s-2}G_k^{z_{i,k}}u^{z_{\xi,i}}=A_{D,i}D_{w_i}^{\chi}.
\]
It then computes
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_0&=\prod_i(b_i g^{-\tilde y_i})^{\rho^{i-1}},\\
P_1&=g^{\sum_i\rho^{i-1}z_{\mu,i}}
      \prod_{k=0}^{s-2}G_k^{\sum_i\rho^{i-1}r_i z_{i,k}},\\
P_2&=\prod_{k=0}^{s-2}G_k^{\sum_i\rho^{i-1}z_{i,k}},
\end{aligned}
\]
and accepts the folded relation iff
\[
    e(P_1P_0^\chi,h)=A_F\cdot e(P_2,h^\tau).
\]
This is a generalized Schnorr protocol for linear relations in the exponent; by the standard special-soundness and honest-verifier zero-knowledge properties, its Fiat--Shamir transform is knowledge-sound and zero-knowledge in the random oracle model.


\subsection{Merkle Tree}
A Merkle tree is a hash-based vector commitment that supports succinct membership authentication. Let $H_1:\{0,1\}^*\rightarrow\{0,1\}^{\lambda}$ be a collision-resistant hash function. FoldAudit uses domain-separated leaves that bind each authenticated tag to its file identifier and chunk index:
\[
    L_{i,j}=H_1(\mathsf{leaf}\|\mathsf{fid}_i\|j\|\mathsf{enc}(\sigma_{i,j})),\qquad j\in[n],
\]
where $\mathsf{fid}_i$ is a canonical file identifier and $\mathsf{enc}(\cdot)$ is an injective group-element encoding. The domain label, file identifier, and index prevent cross-file reuse and position-swapping attacks even when identical tags appear at different positions. Internal nodes are computed recursively with a separate domain label as
$
N=H_1(\mathsf{node}\|N_{\mathsf{left}}\|N_{\mathsf{right}})
$
until a root $\Gamma_i$ is obtained. A membership proof for $\sigma_{i,j}$ consists of the sibling nodes on the path from $L_{i,j}$ to $\Gamma_i$. Verification recomputes the root from $(\mathsf{fid}_i,j,\sigma_{i,j})$ and the path, and accepts only if it equals the trusted root. For a balanced binary tree, each authentication path contains $\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$ hash digests.

\subsection{System Model}
\paragraph{System Entities}
We consider a batch-verifiable Provable Data Possession (PDP) architecture comprising three entities: Data Owner, a Cloud Server and Verifier.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Data Owner} (DO): The DO outsources a batch of $m$ files to the CS for long-term storage. To ensure data integrity without maintaining local copies, the DO delegates periodic auditing to the V.
    \item \textbf{Cloud Server} (CS): The CS is modeled as untrusted and computationally bounded: it may delete, corrupt, or selectively withhold files or chunks to save storage or bandwidth. 
    \item \textbf{Verifier} (V): The V is honest-but-curious. It executes the auditing protocol faithfully but may attempt to infer sensitive information from audit transcripts.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{File Representation}
A file is divided into multiple chunks, and each \textbf{chunk} can be represented through polynomial coefficients. Each coefficient is referred to as a \textbf{sector}.
Let $m$ denote the number of files in a single audit batch, $n$ the number of chunks per file, $s$ the number of sectors per chunk. Namely, each chunk is parsed into $s$ field elements from $\FF_p$ and algebraically encoded as a polynomial of degree at most $s-1$:
$$
    f_{i,j}(X) = \sum_{k=0}^{s-1} m_{i,j,k} X^k, \quad i \in [m], \; j \in [n],
$$
where $m_{i,j,k}$ represents the $k$-th sector of the $j$-th chunk in file $i$. 
A \textbf{tag} is used to represent the \(j\)-th chunk through a KZG polynomial commitment. i.e., $\sigma_{i,j} =  g^{f_{i,j}(\tau)} \in \GG_1$ by Equation~\eqref{eq:kzg_commitment}. Further, tags of each file are organized into a Merkle Tree to guarantee correct indexing and membership.

\paragraph{Protocol Workflow}
The provable data possession scheme for multiple files operates through five polynomial-time algorithms:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\Setup(\lambda) \to \mathsf{pp}$: Generates public parameters $\mathsf{pp}$, including bilinear group tuples $(\GG_1, \GG_2, \GG_T, e)$, independent Pedersen bases $(g,u)$ in $\GG_1$, a structured reference string (SRS) for degree $s-1$, and cryptographic hash functions $H_1$ (for Merkle Tree construction) and $H_2$ (for Fiat-Shamir transcript hashing).
    \item $\Store(\{F_i\}_{i=1}^m) \to (\mathsf{data}, \mathsf{meta})$: The DO encodes each chunk into $f_{i,j}(X)$, computes tags $\sigma_{i,j}$, constructs the indexed Merkle Tree over $L_{i,j}=H_1(\mathsf{leaf}\|\mathsf{fid}_i\|j\|\mathsf{enc}(\sigma_{i,j}))$ to obtain the root hash $\Gamma_i$ for each file, and outsources $\{F_i, \{\sigma_{i,j}\}, \text{Merkle trees}\}$ to the CS. The DO securely records $\{\Gamma_i\}_{i=1}^m$ and shares them with V.
    \item $\Chal(m,n,c) \to \mathsf{chal}$: The verifier samples a shared challenge index set $J \subseteq [n]$ with $|J| = c$, random aggregation coefficients $\{v_{i,j}\}_{i\in[m], j \in J} \leftarrow \FF_p$, and per-file evaluation points $\{r_i\}_{i=1}^m \leftarrow \FF_p$. The challenge is $\mathsf{chal} = (J, \{v_{i,j}\}_{i\in[m],j\in J}, \{r_i\}, nonce)$, where $nonce$ is a random value to resist replay attack.
    \item $\ProofGen(\mathsf{chal}, \mathsf{data}, \mathsf{meta}) \to \pi$: The CS aggregates challenged chunks $J$ and outputs the corresponding non-interactive proofs $\pi$.
    \item $\Verify(\mathsf{chal}, \pi, \{\Gamma_i\}, \mathsf{pp}) \to \{accept, reject\}$: V validates the integrity of all files $[m]$ via the proofs $\pi$.
    % The verifier checks the batched masked-opening proof rather than a public mask-opening equation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Security Model}
\label{sec:security_model}
The security of the proposed batch-PDP scheme is formalized through three core properties: correctness, soundness, and privacy.

\textbf{Correctness}: If all parties (DO, CS, and V) execute the protocol honestly according to the specified algorithms, the $\mathsf{Verify}$ algorithm outputs $accept$ with probability $1$.

\textbf{Soundness}:
Let $\mathcal{A}$ be a probabilistic polynomial-time (PPT) adversary controlling the CS. $\mathcal{A}$ wins the soundness game if it can produce a proof $\pi$ that causes $\Verify$ to output $accept$ for a challenge involving corrupted, modified, or unavailable data chunks with non-negligible probability. 

\textbf{Privacy against Honest-but-Curious Verifier}:
Given the public audit transcript and the generated proof $\pi$, a PPT verifier cannot recover challenged evaluations $\{y_i = f_i(r_i)\}_{i=1}^m$ or the underlying raw data sectors with non-negligible advantage, even when the evaluation domain is small enough to enumerate.


\section{Proposed Scheme}


\subsection{Construction}

\paragraph{\textbf{Setup}} 
The $\Setup$ algorithm initializes the cryptographic environment and publishes the system-wide public parameters $\mathsf{pp}$. It first generates a bilinear group tuple $(\GG_1, \GG_2, \GG_T, e)$ of prime order $p$, selects public generators $g,u \in \GG_1$ with unknown $\log_g u$, selects $h \in \GG_2$, and samples a secret trapdoor $\tau \in \FF_p$. Using $\tau$, the algorithm constructs the structured reference string (SRS) $(g,u, g^{\tau}, \dots, g^{\tau^{s-1}}, h, h^{\tau})$, which supports polynomial commitments of degree strictly less than $s$ and Pedersen mask commitments. Cryptographic hash functions are fixed: $H_1$ for Merkle Tree construction, and $H_2$ for Fiat-Shamir transcript binding. Finally, $\mathsf{pp} = (\GG_1, \GG_2, \GG_T, e, p, g, u, h, \mathsf{SRS}, H_1, H_2)$ is published. Note that each DO has its own trapdoor $\tau$ to enable batch of polynomials and guarantee privacy of DO's files.
% This trusted setup ensures that all subsequent polynomial commitments are evaluation-binding and knowledge-sound under standard hardness assumptions.
\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{$\Setup$}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require security parameter $\lambda$
\Ensure public parameters $pp$
\State Generate $(\GG_1,\GG_2,\GG_T,e,p,g,u,h)$ and $\SRS=\left(g,u,g^\tau,\ldots,g^{\tau^{s-1}},h,h^\tau\right)$.
\State Fix cryptographic hashes $H_1, H_2$.
\State Publish
$
pp=(\GG_1,\GG_2,\GG_T,e,p,g,u,h,\SRS, H_1,H_2).
$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


\paragraph{\textbf{Files Storing}}
The $\Store$ algorithm handles data preprocessing, cryptographic tagging, and metadata generation for outsourcing. For each file $F_i$ ($i \in [m]$), the Data Owner parses every chunk $j \in [n]$ into $s$ field elements and algebraically encodes them as a polynomial $f_{i,j}(X) = \sum_{k=0}^{s-1} m_{i,j,k} X^k$. A KZG homomorphic tag $\sigma_{i,j} = g^{f_{i,j}(\tau)} \in \GG_1$ is computed for each chunk, providing a compact cryptographic binding to the underlying data sectors. Note that \(\tau\) is chosen and known by the data owner, so the computation of tag \(\sigma_{i,j}\) can be performed in constant time. 
Then, each tag is encoded as a domain-separated Merkle leaf $L_{i,j}=H_1(\mathsf{leaf}\|\mathsf{fid}_i\|j\|\mathsf{enc}(\sigma_{i,j}))$, and the leaves are organized into a Merkle Tree with root $\Gamma_i$. The tree roots $\{\Gamma_i\}$ are securely retained by the DO (and later shared with the Verifier), while the raw data and tags are outsourced to the CS. This structure guarantees both data integrity and index authenticity without requiring the DO to maintain local copies.

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{$\Store$}
\label{alg:store}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require files $\{F_i\}_{i=1}^m$
\Ensure outsourced data, metadata
\For{$i=1$ to $m$}
  \For{$j=1$ to $n$}
    \State Parse chunk $j$ into $s$ sectors and form $f_{i,j}(X)$.
    \State Compute tag $\sigma_{i,j}=g^{f_{i,j}(\tau)}$.
    \State $L_{i,j}\gets H_1(\mathsf{leaf}\|\mathsf{fid}_i\|j\|\mathsf{enc}(\sigma_{i,j}))$.
  \EndFor
  \State Build the $i$th Merkle Tree over leaves $\{L_{i,j}\}_{j=1}^n$.
  \State Obtain root $\Gamma_i$.
\EndFor
\State DO sends $(F_i,\{\sigma_{i,j}\})$ to CS and records $\{\Gamma_i\}$ for V.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{\textbf{Challenge Generation}}
The $\mathsf{Chal}$ algorithm initiates the audit by sampling a challenge tuple $\mathsf{chal} = (J, \{v_{i,j}\}_{i\in[m],j\in J}, \{r_i\}_{i=1}^m,\mathsf{nonce})$. An index set $J \subseteq [n]$ of size $c$ is selected and applied to every audited file. Independent coefficients $\{v_{i,j}\}$ are drawn uniformly from $\FF_p$ to randomize each file's linear aggregation and prevent targeted corruption. Independent per-file evaluation points $\{r_i\}$ are sampled to support file-specific quotient checks, while a fresh $\mathsf{nonce}$ prevents replay and precomputation attacks.
\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{$\Chal$}
\label{alg:chal}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require parameters $(m,n,c)$
\Ensure challenge $\mathsf{chal}$
\State Sample one shared challenged index set $J\subseteq[n]$, $|J|=c$.
\State Sample random $\{v_{i,j} \xleftarrow{\$}\FF_p\}_{i\in[m],j\in J}$.
\State Sample random $\{r_i \xleftarrow{\$}\FF_p\}_{i=1}^m$
% \For{$i=1$ to $m$}
%   \State Sample $r_i\xleftarrow{\$}\FF_p$.
% \EndFor
\State Set
$
\mathsf{chal}=(J,\{v_{i,j}\}_{i\in[m],j\in J}, \{r_i\}_{i=1}^m, nonce).
$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}




\paragraph{\textbf{Proof Generation}}

Upon receiving $\mathsf{chal}=(J,\{v_{i,j}\}_{i\in[m],j\in J},\{r_i\}_{i=1}^m,\mathsf{nonce})$, the CS executes $\ProofGen$ independently for each audited file and outputs a fixed proof transcript. The proof contains authenticated challenged tags, one masked evaluation, one Pedersen mask commitment, one aggregated tag per file, and a batched zero-knowledge proof $\Pi_{\mathsf{pm}}$ that binds the hidden masks to the folded KZG quotient relation.


\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{\textbf{$\ProofGen$}}
\label{alg:proofgen}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require challenge $\mathsf{chal}=(J,\{v_{i,j}\}_{i\in[m],j\in J},\{r_i\}_{i=1}^m,\mathsf{nonce})$, files $\{F_i\}_{i=1}^m$, tags $\{\sigma_{i,j}\}$, SRS
\Ensure proof $\pi$
\For{$i=1$ to $m$}
    \Statex {{\color{gray}Step 1: Per-File Tag Authentication Paths Building}}
    \State Build Merkle paths $\mathsf{Auth}_i$ for the leaves $L_{i,j}=H_1(\mathsf{leaf}\|\mathsf{fid}_i\|j\|\mathsf{enc}(\sigma_{i,j}))$, $j\in J$, with respect to $\Gamma_i$
    
    \Statex {{\color{gray}Step 2: Per-File Polynomial Aggregation and Masked Quotient Commitment}}
    \State $f_i(X) \gets \sum_{j\in J} v_{i,j} f_{i,j}(X)=\sum_{k=0}^{s-1} a_{i,k}X^k$
    \State $b_i \gets g^{f_i(\tau)}=\prod_{k=0}^{s-1}(g^{\tau^k})^{a_{i,k}}$
    \State $y_i \gets f_i(r_i)$
    \State Sample $\mu_i \xleftarrow{\$} \FF_p$
    \State Sample $\theta_i \xleftarrow{\$} \FF_p$
    \State $\tilde{y}_i \gets y_i+\mu_i$
    \State $R_i \gets g^{\mu_i}u^{\theta_i}$
    \State $w_i(X) \gets \frac{f_i(X)-y_i}{X-r_i}=\sum_{k=0}^{s-2}d_{i,k}X^k$ \Comment{Polynomial division over $\FF_p$}
    \State Sample $\xi_i \xleftarrow{\$}\FF_p$ and set $D_{w_i}\gets g^{w_i(\tau)}u^{\xi_i}$
\EndFor

\Statex {{\color{gray}Step 3: Fiat-Shamir Sigma Proof}}
\State $\rho \gets H_2(\mathsf{fold},\mathsf{chal},\{\Gamma_i\}_{i=1}^m,\{b_i\}_{i=1}^m,\{\tilde y_i\}_{i=1}^m,\{R_i\}_{i=1}^m,\{D_{w_i}\}_{i=1}^m)$
\State $\mathsf{stmt}\gets(\mathsf{chal},\{\Gamma_i\},\{b_i\},\{\tilde y_i\},\{R_i\},\{D_{w_i}\},\rho)$
\For{$i=1$ to $m$}
    \State Sample $\alpha_i,\beta_i,\gamma_i,\{\delta_{i,k}\}_{k=0}^{s-2}\xleftarrow{\$}\FF_p$
    \State $A_{R,i}\gets g^{\alpha_i}u^{\beta_i}$
    \State $A_{D,i}\gets \prod_{k=0}^{s-2}(g^{\tau^k})^{\delta_{i,k}}u^{\gamma_i}$
\EndFor
\State $P_1^0\gets g^{\sum_i\rho^{i-1}\alpha_i}\prod_{k=0}^{s-2}(g^{\tau^k})^{\sum_i\rho^{i-1}r_i\delta_{i,k}}$
\State $P_2^0\gets \prod_{k=0}^{s-2}(g^{\tau^k})^{\sum_i\rho^{i-1}\delta_{i,k}}$
\State $A_F\gets e(P_1^0,h)\cdot e(P_2^0,h^\tau)^{-1}$
\State $\chi\gets H_2(\mathsf{sigma\mbox{-}pm},\mathsf{stmt},\{A_{R,i},A_{D,i}\}_{i=1}^m,A_F)$
\For{$i=1$ to $m$}
    \State $z_{\mu,i}\gets \alpha_i+\chi\mu_i$, $z_{\theta,i}\gets \beta_i+\chi\theta_i$, $z_{\xi,i}\gets \gamma_i+\chi\xi_i$
    \State $z_{i,k}\gets \delta_{i,k}+\chi d_{i,k}$ for all $0\le k\le s-2$
\EndFor
\State $\Pi_{\mathsf{pm}}\gets\left(A_F,\{A_{R,i},A_{D,i},z_{\mu,i},z_{\theta,i},z_{\xi,i},\{z_{i,k}\}_{k=0}^{s-2}\}_{i=1}^m\right)$

\Statex {{\color{gray}Step 4: Output Proof}}
\State \Return
\[
\pi=
\left(
\left\{
\mathsf{Auth}_i,\tilde{y}_i,R_i,D_{w_i},b_i,\{\sigma_{i,j}\}_{j\in J}
\right\}_{i=1}^m,\Pi_{\mathsf{pm}}
\right).
\]
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}




\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{Step 1: Per-File Tag Authentication Paths Building.} For each file $i \in [m]$, the CS collects the challenged tags $\{\sigma_{i,j}\}_{j\in J}$ and builds Merkle authentication paths for the bound leaves $L_{i,j}=H_1(\mathsf{leaf}\|\mathsf{fid}_i\|j\|\mathsf{enc}(\sigma_{i,j}))$ with respect to the root $\Gamma_i$.
    \item \textit{Step 2: Per-File Aggregation and Masked Evaluation.} The CS aggregates the challenged chunks into
    $
    f_i(X)=\sum_{j\in J} v_{i,j}f_{i,j}(X)
    $
    and computes the aggregate commitment directly from the coefficient vector of $f_i(X)$:
    $
    b_i=g^{f_i(\tau)}=\prod_{k=0}^{s-1}(g^{\tau^k})^{a_{i,k}},
    $
    where $f_i(X)=\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}a_{i,k}X^k$. This avoids exponentiating the $c$ challenged tags in proof generation; the equality $b_i=\prod_{j\in J}\sigma_{i,j}^{v_{i,j}}$ is enforced later by the verifier. It then evaluates $y_i=f_i(r_i)$, samples fresh $\mu_i,\theta_i\leftarrow\FF_p$, outputs the masked value $\tilde{y}_i=y_i+\mu_i$, and commits to the mask with the Pedersen commitment $R_i=g^{\mu_i}u^{\theta_i}$.
    \item \textit{Step 3: Per-File Quotient Witnesses and Batched Masked Opening.} Since $y_i=f_i(r_i)$, the polynomial $f_i(X)-y_i$ is divisible by $X-r_i$. The CS computes
    $
    w_i(X)=\frac{f_i(X)-y_i}{X-r_i}
    $
    with $\deg(w_i)\le s-2$, samples $\xi_i\leftarrow\FF_p$, and publishes the hiding quotient commitment $D_{w_i}=g^{w_i(\tau)}u^{\xi_i}$. The Fiat-Shamir scalar $\rho$ is derived after all $D_{w_i}$ are fixed and is used inside the batched masked-opening proof. No value equivalent to $g^{\mu_i}$ or the unblinded quotient commitment $g^{w_i(\tau)}$ is published.
    \item \textit{Step 4: Output.} The proof is exactly
    $
    \pi=\left(\left\{\mathsf{Auth}_i,\tilde{y}_i,R_i,D_{w_i},b_i,\{\sigma_{i,j}\}_{j\in J}\right\}_{i=1}^m,\Pi_{\mathsf{pm}}\right).
    $
\end{enumerate}


\paragraph{\textbf{Verification}}

The $\Verify$ algorithm validates index authenticity, tag aggregation, transcript binding, and quotient consistency. The quotient consistency check is performed by verifying $\Pi_{\mathsf{pm}}$, which proves the folded masked KZG relation without exposing the mask exponents.


\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\textbf{$\Verify$}}
\label{alg:verify}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require challenge $\mathsf{chal}=(J,\{v_{i,j}\}_{i\in[m],j\in J},\{r_i\}_{i=1}^m,\mathsf{nonce})$, proof $\pi$, Merkle roots $\{\Gamma_i\}_{i=1}^m$, public parameters $\mathsf{pp}$
\Ensure $\mathsf{accept}$ or $\mathsf{reject}$

\State Parse $\pi$ as
\[
\pi=
\left(
\left\{
\mathsf{Auth}_i,\tilde{y}_i,R_i,D_{w_i},b_i,\{\sigma_{i,j}\}_{j\in J}
\right\}_{i=1}^m,\Pi_{\mathsf{pm}}
\right).
\]

\Statex {{\color{gray}Step 1: Tag Authentication}}
\For{$i=1$ to $m$}
    \State Verify $\mathsf{Auth}_i$ against $\Gamma_i$ for the leaves $L_{i,j}=H_1(\mathsf{leaf}\|\mathsf{fid}_i\|j\|\mathsf{enc}(\sigma_{i,j}))$, $j\in J$
    \If{verification fails}
        \State \Return $\mathsf{reject}$
    \EndIf
\EndFor

\Statex {{\color{gray}Step 2: Per-File Tag Consistency}}
\For{$i=1$ to $m$}
    \State $\hat{b}_i \gets \prod_{j\in J} \sigma_{i,j}^{v_{i,j}}$
    \If{$b_i \neq \hat{b}_i$}
        \State \Return $\mathsf{reject}$
    \EndIf
\EndFor

\Statex {{\color{gray}Step 3: Fiat-Shamir Folding Challenge}}
\State $\rho \gets H_2(\mathsf{fold},\mathsf{chal},\{\Gamma_i\}_{i=1}^m,\{b_i\}_{i=1}^m,\{\tilde{y}_i\}_{i=1}^m,\{R_i\}_{i=1}^m,\{D_{w_i}\}_{i=1}^m)$
\State $\mathsf{stmt}\gets(\mathsf{chal},\{\Gamma_i\},\{b_i\},\{\tilde y_i\},\{R_i\},\{D_{w_i}\},\rho)$

\Statex {{\color{gray}Step 4: Sigma Proof Verification}}
\State Parse $\Pi_{\mathsf{pm}}$ as $\left(A_F,\{A_{R,i},A_{D,i},z_{\mu,i},z_{\theta,i},z_{\xi,i},\{z_{i,k}\}_{k=0}^{s-2}\}_{i=1}^m\right)$
\State $\chi\gets H_2(\mathsf{sigma\mbox{-}pm},\mathsf{stmt},\{A_{R,i},A_{D,i}\}_{i=1}^m,A_F)$
\For{$i=1$ to $m$}
    \If{$g^{z_{\mu,i}}u^{z_{\theta,i}}\neq A_{R,i}R_i^\chi$}
        \State \Return $\mathsf{reject}$
    \EndIf
    \If{$\prod_{k=0}^{s-2}(g^{\tau^k})^{z_{i,k}}u^{z_{\xi,i}}\neq A_{D,i}D_{w_i}^{\chi}$}
        \State \Return $\mathsf{reject}$
    \EndIf
\EndFor
\State $P_0\gets\prod_i(b_i g^{-\tilde y_i})^{\rho^{i-1}}$
\State $P_1\gets g^{\sum_i\rho^{i-1}z_{\mu,i}}\prod_{k=0}^{s-2}(g^{\tau^k})^{\sum_i\rho^{i-1}r_i z_{i,k}}$
\State $P_2\gets\prod_{k=0}^{s-2}(g^{\tau^k})^{\sum_i\rho^{i-1}z_{i,k}}$
\If{$e(P_1P_0^\chi,h)\neq A_F\cdot e(P_2,h^\tau)$}
    \State \Return $\mathsf{reject}$
\EndIf

\State \Return $\mathsf{accept}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{Step 1: Tag Authentication.} For every file, the verifier checks $\mathsf{Auth}_i$ against the trusted Merkle root $\Gamma_i$ for the challenged leaves $L_{i,j}=H_1(\mathsf{leaf}\|\mathsf{fid}_i\|j\|\mathsf{enc}(\sigma_{i,j}))$. This prevents the CS from substituting tags across files or chunk positions.
    \item \textit{Step 2: Per-File Tag Consistency.} The verifier recomputes
    $
    \hat{b}_i=\prod_{j\in J}\sigma_{i,j}^{v_{i,j}}
    $
    and rejects unless $\hat{b}_i=b_i$ for every $i\in[m]$. Hence each $b_i$ is forced to be the homomorphic commitment to the challenged aggregate polynomial $f_i(X)$.
    \item \textit{Step 3: Fiat--Shamir Folding Challenge.} The verifier derives
    $
    \rho=H_2(\mathsf{fold},\mathsf{chal},\{\Gamma_i\},\{b_i\},\{\tilde{y}_i\},\{R_i\},\{D_{w_i}\})
    $
    after all public proof components have been parsed. Since $\rho$ is bound to the fixed transcript, the prover cannot adapt the quotient witnesses to the folding coefficient.
    \item \textit{Step 4: Pedersen Masked-Opening Proof Verification.} The verifier checks $\Pi_{\mathsf{pm}}$ for $\mathcal{R}_{\mathsf{pm}}$. Acceptance means that the prover knows masks and Pedersen blindings satisfying $R_i=g^{\mu_i}u^{\theta_i}$, hiding quotient openings satisfying $D_{w_i}=g^{w_i(\tau)}u^{\xi_i}$, and quotient witnesses $w_i(X)$ satisfying the folded KZG relation for the hidden values $y_i=\tilde y_i-\mu_i$. Thus the verifier learns no checkable group element of the form $g^{\mu_i}$.
\end{enumerate}
If all checks pass, the verifier outputs $accept$; otherwise, it outputs $reject$.

\subsection{Security Analysis}
We establish correctness, soundness, and privacy for the protocol described in Algorithms~\ref{alg:store}--\ref{alg:verify}. All adversaries considered below are probabilistic polynomial-time (PPT) algorithms in the security parameter $\lambda$. A function $\varepsilon(\lambda)$ is negligible if it is smaller than $1/\lambda^c$ for every constant $c>0$ and all sufficiently large $\lambda$. The analysis relies on the following assumptions.

\begin{assumption}[Collision Resistance of $H_1$]\label{ass:hash}
The Merkle-tree hash function $H_1:\{0,1\}^*\rightarrow\{0,1\}^{\lambda}$ is collision-resistant. Namely, for every PPT adversary $\mathcal{A}$,
\[
    \Pr\!\left[
    x\neq x' \land H_1(x)=H_1(x')
    : (x,x')\leftarrow\mathcal{A}(1^\lambda)
    \right]\le \mathsf{negl}(\lambda).
\]
Consequently, a PPT adversary cannot produce two distinct tag-index sets that authenticate to the same Merkle root except with negligible probability.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Fiat--Shamir Random Oracle]\label{ass:random_oracle}
The transcript hash $H_2$ is modeled as a programmable random oracle with domain separation for masked-opening proofs and proof folding. In particular, once the tuple $(\mathsf{chal},\{\Gamma_i\},\{b_i\},\{\tilde y_i\},\{R_i\},\{D_{w_i}\})$ is fixed, the value
\[
    \rho=H_2(\mathsf{fold},\mathsf{chal},\{\Gamma_i\},\{b_i\},\{\tilde y_i\},\{R_i\},\{D_{w_i}\})
\]
is distributed uniformly over $\FF_p$ from the adversary's perspective, except with negligible probability.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[KZG Binding and Knowledge Soundness]\label{ass:kzg_soundness}
For the SRS degree bound $q\ge s-1$, the $q$-SDH assumption holds in the bilinear group tuple $(\GG_1,\GG_2,\GG_T,e)$. Equivalently for our use, the KZG scheme instantiated with the KZG SRS $(g,g^\tau,\ldots,g^{\tau^q},h,h^\tau)$ is computationally evaluation-binding and knowledge-sound: for every PPT adversary that outputs $(C,r,y,\pi)$ satisfying the KZG verification equation, there exists an extractor that recovers a degree-$\le q$ polynomial $f(X)$ such that $C=g^{f(\tau)}$ and $f(r)=y$, except with negligible probability. In particular, no PPT adversary can open a fixed commitment at a fixed point to an incorrect or second distinct value with non-negligible probability.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Discrete Logarithm Hardness]\label{ass:discrete_log}
The discrete logarithm problem is hard in $\GG_1$. That is, for every PPT adversary $\mathcal{A}$,
\[
    \Pr\!\left[y'=y:
    y\leftarrow\FF_p,\; Y=g^y,\; y'\leftarrow\mathcal{A}(g,Y)
    \right]\le \mathsf{negl}(\lambda).
\]
This assumption is used for computational binding of Pedersen commitments and for the standard hardness of extracting committed exponents.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Pedersen Binding]\label{ass:pedersen_binding}
The discrete logarithm $\log_g u$ is unknown to every PPT adversary. Consequently, Pedersen commitments $g^a u^b$ are perfectly hiding and computationally binding under the discrete logarithm assumption in $\GG_1$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Masked-Opening Sigma Soundness]\label{ass:mask_opening}
The Fiat--Shamir Sigma proof $\Pi_{\mathsf{pm}}$ used in Algorithms~\ref{alg:proofgen} and~\ref{alg:verify} is knowledge-sound and zero-knowledge for the relation $\mathcal{R}_{\mathsf{pm}}$ in the random oracle model. Equivalently, any PPT prover that makes the Sigma verification equations in Algorithm~\ref{alg:verify} accept admits an extractor that outputs witnesses $\{\mu_i,\theta_i,\xi_i,w_i(X)\}_{i=1}^m$ satisfying $R_i=g^{\mu_i}u^{\theta_i}$, $D_{w_i}=g^{w_i(\tau)}u^{\xi_i}$, and the folded masked KZG quotient relation, except with negligible probability.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Correctness]\label{thm:correctness}
If the Data Owner, Cloud Server, and Verifier execute Algorithms~\ref{alg:store},~\ref{alg:chal},~\ref{alg:proofgen}, and~\ref{alg:verify} honestly, then $\Verify$ outputs $accept$ with probability $1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each file $i\in[m]$, the honest CS computes
$
f_i(X)=\sum_{j\in J}v_{i,j}f_{i,j}(X)
$
and $y_i=f_i(r_i)$. Hence $X-r_i$ divides $f_i(X)-y_i$, and
$
w_i(X)=\frac{f_i(X)-y_i}{X-r_i}
$
is a polynomial of degree at most $s-2$.

The Merkle paths $\mathsf{Auth}_i$ are generated from the challenged bound leaves $L_{i,j}=H_1(\mathsf{leaf}\|\mathsf{fid}_i\|j\|\mathsf{enc}(\sigma_{i,j}))$, so Step 1 of $\Verify$ accepts. For Step 2,
\[
\hat b_i=\prod_{j\in J}\sigma_{i,j}^{v_{i,j}}
=\prod_{j\in J}\left(g^{f_{i,j}(\tau)}\right)^{v_{i,j}}
=g^{f_i(\tau)}
=b_i .
\]
The Pedersen commitments satisfy $R_i=g^{\mu_i}u^{\theta_i}$ and $D_{w_i}=g^{w_i(\tau)}u^{\xi_i}$ by construction. Let $C_{w_i}=g^{w_i(\tau)}$ denote the unblinded quotient commitment used only inside the proof witness. For the folded masked relation, the honest witnesses satisfy
\begin{align*}
b_ig^{\mu_i}g^{-\tilde y_i}C_{w_i}^{r_i}
&=g^{f_i(\tau)}g^{\mu_i}g^{-(y_i+\mu_i)}g^{r_iw_i(\tau)}\\
&=g^{f_i(\tau)-y_i+r_iw_i(\tau)}
=g^{\tau w_i(\tau)} .
\end{align*}
Therefore the folded KZG equation inside $\mathcal{R}_{\mathsf{pm}}$ holds, and the concrete Sigma verification equations in Algorithm~\ref{alg:verify} accept by completeness. Thus every verification step accepts.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Soundness]\label{thm:soundness}
Let $\mathcal{A}$ be a PPT adversary controlling the CS. Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:hash}--\ref{ass:mask_opening}, if a challenge touches corrupted, modified, or unavailable chunks, then $\mathcal{A}$ causes $\Verify$ to accept without possessing the challenged aggregate data with probability at most
\[
\frac{m-1}{p}+\mathsf{negl}(\lambda).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Merkle verification binds each challenged tag $\sigma_{i,j}$ to $(\mathsf{fid}_i,j,\Gamma_i)$ except with negligible probability under Assumption~\ref{ass:hash}. The tag-consistency step then forces
$
b_i=\prod_{j\in J}\sigma_{i,j}^{v_{i,j}}=g^{f_i(\tau)}
$
for the authenticated aggregate polynomial $f_i(X)$.

By Assumption~\ref{ass:mask_opening}, any accepting $\Pi_{\mathsf{pm}}$ yields witnesses $\{\mu_i,\theta_i,\xi_i,w_i(X)\}_{i=1}^m$ satisfying $R_i=g^{\mu_i}u^{\theta_i}$ and $D_{w_i}=g^{w_i(\tau)}u^{\xi_i}$. Let $C_{w_i}=g^{w_i(\tau)}$ and $y_i=\tilde y_i-\mu_i$. Since $D_{w_i}$ is fixed before $\rho$ is sampled, the extracted quotient commitments are fixed before $\rho$ except with negligible probability under Assumptions~\ref{ass:random_oracle} and~\ref{ass:pedersen_binding}. If the masked-opening proof accepts, then the folded relation enforces
\[
\sum_{i=1}^m \rho^{i-1}
\left(f_i(\tau)-y_i-(\tau-r_i)w_i(\tau)\right)=0 .
\]
For each file define the opening defect
\[
\Delta_i=f_i(\tau)-y_i-(\tau-r_i)w_i(\tau).
\]
If every $\Delta_i=0$, then each $C_{w_i}$ is a valid KZG witness opening $b_i$ at $r_i$ to $y_i$. Producing such witnesses for corrupted or unavailable challenged data without the corresponding aggregate polynomial violates KZG knowledge soundness except with negligible probability.

Otherwise, at least one $\Delta_i$ is nonzero. By Assumption~\ref{ass:random_oracle}, $\rho$ is uniform after all values $(b_i,\tilde y_i,R_i,D_{w_i})$ are fixed, so the adversary must make the nonzero polynomial
\[
P_\Delta(\rho)=\sum_{i=1}^m \Delta_i\rho^{i-1}
\]
vanish at a random field point. This polynomial has degree at most $m-1$, so by Lemma~\ref{lem:schwartz_zippel},
$
\Pr[P_\Delta(\rho)=0]\le (m-1)/p.
$
Combining the negligible tag/KZG failures with the folding error gives the claimed bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Privacy against Honest-but-Curious Verifier]\label{thm:privacy}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:discrete_log},~\ref{ass:pedersen_binding}, and~\ref{ass:mask_opening}, if every mask $\mu_i$ and blinding scalar $\theta_i$ are freshly sampled from $\FF_p$, then an honest-but-curious verifier cannot recover the challenged evaluations $\{y_i=f_i(r_i)\}_{i=1}^m$ or the raw sectors $\{m_{i,j,k}\}$ from the audit transcript with non-negligible advantage, even when the candidate space for $y_i$ is small.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The field element $\tilde y_i=y_i+\mu_i$ is statistically uniform over $\FF_p$ because $\mu_i$ is uniform and used once. The accompanying commitment $R_i=g^{\mu_i}u^{\theta_i}$ is a Pedersen commitment; for any candidate value $y'$ and corresponding candidate mask $\mu'=\tilde y_i-y'$, there exists a blinding value $\theta'$ such that $R_i=g^{\mu'}u^{\theta'}$. Hence $R_i$ gives no testable predicate for distinguishing the true $y_i$ from any candidate.

The hiding quotient commitment $D_{w_i}=g^{w_i(\tau)}u^{\xi_i}$ is also statistically hiding, and $\Pi_{\mathsf{pm}}$ is zero-knowledge by Assumption~\ref{ass:mask_opening}. Therefore the verifier's view can be simulated from the public metadata, challenged tags, and roots without knowing $\{y_i,\mu_i,\theta_i,\xi_i,w_i(X)\}$. Recovering raw sector coefficients from $\sigma_{i,j}$ or $b_i$ would require solving discrete logarithms or learning the trapdoor $\tau$. Thus the transcript does not enable recovery of the challenged evaluations or raw sectors, except with negligible probability.
\end{proof}



\subsection{Complexity Analysis}
\label{sec:complexity_analysis}
We measure computational overhead in terms of group exponentiations ($E$) and bilinear pairings ($P$). Field arithmetic, hash evaluations, Fiat--Shamir transcript hashing, group multiplications, and Merkle-path hashing are omitted from the group-operation count. Communication overhead is measured in bits using $|p|$, $|\GG_1|$, $|\GG_T|$, and $|h|$ for a field element, a $\GG_1$ element, a target-group element, and a hash digest, respectively. The following counts instantiate $\Pi_{\mathsf{pm}}$ with the concrete Sigma protocol in Section~\ref{sec:multi_poly_batch_evaluation}.

\paragraph{Computation Complexity.} 
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\Store$ Algorithm. Since the DO holds the trapdoor $\tau$, each tag $\sigma_{i,j}=g^{f_{i,j}(\tau)}$ costs one exponentiation after evaluating $f_{i,j}(\tau)$ in the field. Across $m$ files and $n$ chunks, the storage-side group cost is
    \[
        mnE .
    \]
    \item $\ProofGen$ Algorithm. For each file, after forming the aggregated polynomial $f_i(X)=\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}a_{i,k}X^k$ in the field, the CS computes the aggregate commitment as $b_i=g^{f_i(\tau)}=\prod_{k=0}^{s-1}(g^{\tau^k})^{a_{i,k}}$ using the SRS. This costs $sE$, rather than $cE$, and is equivalent to $\prod_{j\in J}\sigma_{i,j}^{v_{i,j}}$ by the homomorphism of KZG commitments. The CS also computes the Pedersen mask commitment $R_i=g^{\mu_i}u^{\theta_i}$ using $2E$ and the hiding quotient commitment $D_{w_i}=g^{w_i(\tau)}u^{\xi_i}$ using $sE$. Thus the explicit per-file commitments cost
    \[
        \underbrace{msE}_{\{b_i\}}+
        \underbrace{2mE}_{\{R_i\}}+
        \underbrace{msE}_{\{D_{w_i}\}}
        =
        m(2s+2)E .
    \]
    The Sigma commitments add $2mE$ for $\{A_{R,i}\}$, $msE$ for $\{A_{D,i}\}$, $(2s-1)E$ for $(P_1^0,P_2^0)$, and $2P$ for $A_F$. Hence the full proof-generation group cost is
    \[
        (3ms+4m+2s-1)E+2P .
    \]
    Since the audit challenge size satisfies $c>s$ in the intended parameter regime, computing $b_i$ from the coefficient vector of $f_i$ keeps proof generation at $O(msE)+2P$ rather than $O(mcE)$.
    \item $\Verify$ Algorithm. The verifier recomputes $\hat b_i=\prod_{j\in J}\sigma_{i,j}^{v_{i,j}}$ for all files using $mcE$. The Sigma proof then costs $3mE$ for the $R_i$ opening checks, $(s+1)mE$ for the $D_{w_i}$ opening checks, and $(m+2s+1)E+2P$ for $(P_0,P_1,P_2)$ and the folded pairing check. Therefore verification costs
    \[
        (mc+ms+5m+2s+1)E+2P .
    \]
\end{itemize}





\paragraph{Communication Complexity.}


\begin{itemize}
    \item $\Store$ Algorithm. The DO transmits raw file data to the CS, incurring $mns|p|$ bits. Concurrently, $mn$ homomorphic tags $\{\sigma_{i,j}\}$ are outsourced to enable future homomorphic aggregation, costing $mn|\GG_1|$ bits. The DO securely shares $m$ Merkle root hashes $\{\Gamma_i\}_{i=1}^m$ with the verifier for audit authentication, requiring $m|h|$ bits. The total one-time outsourcing communication is $mns|p| + mn|\GG_1| + m|h|$.
    \[
    \begin{aligned}
    &\underbrace{mns|p|}_{\text{File data}}+
    \underbrace{mn|\GG_1|}_{\text{Tags}}+
    \underbrace{m|h|}_{\text{Merkle roots}}\\
    &=mns|p|+mn|\GG_1|+m|h| .
    \end{aligned}
    \]
    \item $\ProofGen$ Algorithm. For each file $i\in[m]$, the proof contains $c$ challenged tags $\{\sigma_{i,j}\}_{j\in J}$, three group elements $(R_i,D_{w_i},b_i)$, one field element $\tilde y_i$, and $c$ Merkle authentication paths. The Sigma proof contributes two additional $\GG_1$ elements $(A_{R,i},A_{D,i})$ per file, $(s+2)$ field responses per file, and one target-group element $A_F$. Therefore the total proof communication is
    \[
        m(c+5)|\GG_1|+m(s+3)|p|+|\GG_T|+mc\log_2 n|h| .
    \]
\end{itemize}






\subsection{Dynamic File Management}
The scheme natively supports file addition and deletion through independent per-file KZG commitments and modular Merkle trees. Crucially, $\ProofGen$ and $\Verify$ are stateless per audit round; they operate on the current active file set without maintaining persistent or incremental proof state. Dynamic operations thus only affect the $\Store$ phase and metadata synchronization.


When the DO uploads a new file $F_{m+1}$, it encodes each chunk into $f_{m+1,j}(X)$, computes tags $\sigma_{m+1,j}=g^{f_{m+1,j}(\tau)}$, and constructs a fresh Merkle tree yielding root $\Gamma_{m+1}$. The data and tags are outsourced to the CS, while $\Gamma_{m+1}$ is securely shared with the verifier. The active batch size increments to $m \leftarrow m+1$. Subsequent audits automatically incorporate $F_{m+1}$ via the expanded index collection and Merkle root set.


To securely remove $F_{i_{\text{del}}}$, the CS permanently erases the associated data, tags, and Merkle nodes. The DO and V discard $\Gamma_{i_{\text{del}}}$. Surviving files simply skip the removed index in subsequent audit loops, incurring zero recomputation of tags or Merkle roots.
The batch size decrements to $m \leftarrow m-1$, and future $\ProofGen$ executions simply iterate over the reduced active set.

\textit{Complexity of Dynamic Operations.} Adding or deleting a single file requires $\mathcal{O}(nE)$ time for tag generation, as implied by the \textsf{Store} algorithm. Importantly, no SRS regeneration, trapdoor rotation, or global commitment recomputation is needed. Since proof generation is challenge-driven and stateless, dynamic updates incur overhead strictly at the storage and metadata layers. Consequently, the \textsf{ProofGen} and \textsf{Verify} algorithms retain the same complexity as analyzed in Section~\ref{sec:complexity_analysis}.

\section{Related Works}
\label{sec:related_works}


Recent years have witnessed significant advances in provable data possession (PDP) schemes for cloud and decentralized storage systems. The works most closely related to FoldAudit differ in their batching model, privacy treatment, and support for dynamic data.

Xu et al.~\cite{XuTIFS26} proposed MPA, a lightweight and updatable auditing scheme that combines polynomial commitments with Merkle authentication for decentralized multi-copy storage. MPA supports full aggregation across files and providers and uses a blockchain-assisted commit-and-reveal procedure to derive unpredictable challenges. Its proof-generation cost is dominated by a degree-$(s-1)$ KZG witness, while the Merkle layer gives efficient block-level updates. However, the multi-file verification equation aggregates all local proofs at a shared evaluation point $r$. Therefore, the batch check does not provide independent per-file evaluation points or an explicit random-folding soundness bound for cross-file cancellation; it verifies a globally aggregated opening together with Merkle membership paths.

Zhang et al.~\cite{ZhangTPDS23} addressed on-chain overhead for decentralized multi-copy auditing by instantiating the PCS-MPAP batch polynomial commitment. Their batch scheme assigns distinct evaluation points $\{r_l\}_{l=1}^d$ to files and publishes a constant-size per-SSP proof $(\sigma'_i,W_i,W'_i,E_i)$. This construction substantially reduces on-chain storage and avoids linear growth in the number of files. Nevertheless, the batch proof exposes the scalar $E_i=\sum_{l=1}^d \beta_l S_{l,i}$, i.e., an unmasked linear combination of file evaluation claims. On a transparent ledger, such plaintext evaluation combinations do not provide statistical evaluation privacy against an honest-but-curious verifier. The scheme also focuses on multi-copy auditing rather than dynamic file updates.

Yu et al.~\cite{YuTC25} designed a storage verification scheme for resource-constrained Industrial IoT environments. It combines a homomorphic hash function, polynomial commitments, and a Tag-IMHT authenticated structure to eliminate pairings from verification and to support chunk-level modification, insertion, and deletion. The privacy-enhanced variant masks the proof polynomial by adding a random term derived from $(\beta,\eta)$ and returns an additional masking tag $B$. This protects the challenged polynomial from interpolation attacks in the single-file setting. The scheme, however, does not provide a native multi-file batch verification equation; auditing multiple files requires independent protocol executions.

Miao et al.~\cite{MiaoSCIS2026} proposed MKPDP, a blockchain-assisted multi-keyword searchable PDP scheme. Its index matrix and match indexes let the verifier check that all files matching the queried keywords participate in the audit, thereby addressing search-result integrity, pre-matching, and pre-computation attacks. The proof $\{T,\mu_1,R\}$ aggregates authenticators over the matched files and masks the aggregate data value as $\mu_1=\mu-rh_1(T_w,R)$. Thus, MKPDP supports keyword-filtered multi-file auditing and data privacy, but it is not a polynomial-opening batch protocol and does not provide independent per-file evaluation points or a tight finite-field folding bound.

Despite these advances, existing schemes leave at least one of the following gaps: no native arbitrary multi-file batch audit, no independent per-file polynomial challenge points, no explicit tight batch soundness bound, limited evaluation privacy on transparent ledgers, or dynamic support with a different update granularity. FoldAudit addresses these gaps through Fiat--Shamir folding over independently challenged per-file quotient equations, transcript-bound masked evaluations, and stateless file-level add/delete operations.

\noindent\textit{2.1) Computation Complexity of Zhang et al.~\cite{ZhangTPDS23}}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$\Store$ Algorithm.}  For each file block, the tag \(\sigma_{i,j}=(H(F_{id}\|i\|j)\cdot g^{f_{i,j}(\alpha)})^x\) requires one exponentiation for \(g^{f_{i,j}(\alpha)}\) and one exponentiation for
the outer secret-key power. Across \(mn\) tags this gives \(2mnE\). 
% During storage-phase tag verification, the provider recomputes \(g^{f_{i,j}(\alpha)}\) from the \(s\) sector coefficients and checks a two-pairing equation for each tag; this adds \(mnsE+2mnP\).  Thus the storage computation is \((2mn+mns)E+2mnP\).
\item \textbf{$\ProofGen$ Algorithm.} For each file, exponentiating the \(c\) challenged tags by their audit coefficients costs \(cE\), and applying the file-folding
coefficient \(\beta_l\) costs one more exponentiation.  Over \(m\) files this contributes \(mcE+mE\). The two polynomial-commitment proof elements \(W\) and \(W'\) are obtained from coefficient vectors of length \(s-1\), so their counted work is \((2s-2)E\). Therefore, the total cost is \((mc+m+2s-2)E\).
\item \textbf{$\Verify$ Algorithm.} The verifier constructs the challenge term \(\zeta\) by exponentiating \(c\) hashed indices per file and then applying \(m\) file-folding powers, costing \((mc+m)E\). It also forms \(\psi=g^{-E_1}\cdot W_1^{-Z_T(z)}\) and \(u\cdot v^{-z}\), which contributes \(3E\).  The batch equation \(e(\sigma',g)e(\psi,v)=e(\zeta,v)e(W',u\cdot v^{-z})\) contains four pairings. Hence, verification costs \((mc+m+3)E+4P\).
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textit{2.2) Communication Complexity of Zhang et al.~\cite{ZhangTPDS23}}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$\Store$ Algorithm.}  The storage phase sends the file copy, containing \(mns\) field sectors, and \(mn\) tags. The storage communication is \(mns|p|+mn|G_1|\).
\item \textbf{$\ProofGen$ Algorithm.}  The published proof is
\((\sigma',W_1,W'_1,E_1)\), namely three group elements and one field element.  Therefore the audit-proof communication is \(3|G_1|+|p|\).
\end{itemize}



\noindent\textit{3.1) Computation Complexity of Miao et al.~\cite{MiaoSCIS2026}}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$\Store$ Algorithm.}  In IndexGen, the data owner computes
one match index \(\Omega_i=(H_{F_i}\cdot H_2(FID_i))^{-x}\) per file, so
the counted work is \(mE\).  In AuthGen, the data owner creates one
authenticator
\(\sigma_{i,j}=(H_{F_i}\cdot H_2(FID_i)\cdot H_3(au_{i,j})\cdot
u^{m_{i,j}})^x\) for each block.  Following the same convention as the
scheme's algorithmic cost model, this contributes one counted group
exponentiation per authenticator after the hash and multiplication
terms are omitted, giving \(mnE\).  Therefore the full storage-side
computation is \((m+mn)E\), with no pairing.  If the keyword-index module
is stripped and only the PDP authentication core is compared, this phase
reduces to \(mnE\).
\item \textbf{$\ProofGen$ Algorithm.}  After locating the matched set of
\(m\) files, the server computes
\(T=\prod_{i\in R}\prod_{j\in Q}\sigma_{i,j}^{e_{i,j}}\), which costs
\(mcE\), and generates the privacy element \(R_g=g^r\), which costs
one more exponentiation.  Thus the proof-generation computation is
\((mc+1)E\).
\item \textbf{$\Verify$ Algorithm.}  The verifier exponentiates one
match index \(\Omega_i\) for each matched file and \(c\) hash terms
inside each matched file, and it also computes \(u^{\mu_1}\) and
\(R_g^{h_1(T_w,R_g)}\).  These operations contribute
\((m+mc+2)E\).  The final verification equation has two pairings,
so the verifier computation is \((m(c+1)+2)E+2P\).
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textit{3.2) Communication Complexity of Miao et al.~\cite{MiaoSCIS2026}}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$\Store$ Algorithm.}  The data and authenticators sent to
the cloud contribute \(mn|p|+mn|G_1|\).  The match indexes contain one
group element per file, adding \(m|G_1|\).  Thus the normalized storage
communication is \(mn|p|+(mn+m)|G_1|\), excluding auxiliary searchable
index metadata such as addresses and encrypted index matrices.
\item \textbf{$\ProofGen$ Algorithm.}  The audit proof
\((T,\mu_1,R_g)\) consists of two group elements and one field element,
so the proof communication is \(|p|+2|G_1|\).
\end{itemize}


\noindent\textit{4.1) Computation Complexity of Xu et al.~\cite{XuTIFS26}}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$\Store$ Algorithm.}  For each file and block, the data
owner forms the block polynomial and computes the tag
\(\sigma_{i,j}=g^{f_{m_{i,j}}(\alpha)}\).  Since polynomial evaluation
uses field operations, tag generation contributes one group
exponentiation for each of the \(mn\) tags.  Hashing the Merkle leaves
and roots is omitted under the current computation metric.  The
storage-side \textsf{TagVerify} cost reported by XuTIFS26 is a separate
validation phase and is not included in this Store/Setup column.  Thus
the normalized storage computation is \(mnE\), with no pairing.
\item \textbf{$\ProofGen$ Algorithm.}  For each file, the CSP linearly
combines the \(c\) challenged block polynomials, evaluates the
aggregate polynomial at the challenge point, and divides by \(X-r\).
These are field and polynomial operations.  The only counted operation is
the KZG witness \(w_i=g^{h_i(\alpha)}\).  Since \(h_i(X)\) has degree at
most \(s-2\), computing the witness from the structured public key costs
\((s-1)E\) per local proof.  Across \(m\) local proofs, proof generation
costs \(m(s-1)E\), with no pairing.
\item \textbf{$\Verify$ Algorithm.}  The verifier first aggregates the
challenged tags by computing
\(\prod_{j=1}^{c}\sigma_{i,a_j}^{v_j}\) for every file, which costs
\(mcE\).  It then computes \(g^{val^{global}}\) and
\(g^{\alpha-r}\), adding \(2E\).  The accepted batch path checks one
global KZG equation, requiring the two pairings
\(\hat e(\sigma^{global}/g^{val^{global}},g)\) and
\(\hat e(w^{global},g^{\alpha-r})\).  Thus verification costs
\((mc+2)E+2P\).  The fallback diagnosis used only after a failed global
check is not included in this normal verification cost.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textit{4.2) Communication Complexity of Xu et al.~\cite{XuTIFS26}}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$\Store$ Algorithm.}  The storage phase sends \(mn\)
encrypted blocks, each with \(s\) field sectors, and \(mn\) polynomial
tags.  The Merkle root for each file is published as the
verification anchor, and the algorithm also supplies authentication paths
for stored blocks to the corresponding CSP.  Therefore the one-time
storage communication is
\(mns|p|+mn|G_1|+mn(\log_2 n+1)|h|+m|h|\).  If the server reconstructs and
stores the Merkle tree locally instead of receiving all paths, the
\(mn(\log_2 n+1)|h|\) term can be omitted.
\item \textbf{$\ProofGen$ Algorithm.}  For each file, the proof
contains one evaluation value \(val_i\), one witness \(w_i\), the \(c\)
challenged tags, and \(c\) Merkle authentication paths.  Across \(m\)
proofs, the audit-proof communication is
\(m\bigl(|p|+(c+1)|G_1|+c\log_2 n|h|\bigr)\).
\end{itemize}

\section{Evaluation}

\begin{lemma}[Overall Acceptance Probability Bound]
Let $\rho \in [0,1]$ denote the fraction of corrupted or missing chunks in any audited file $F_i$, and let $d = \lfloor \rho n \rfloor$. When the Cloud Server executes $\ProofGen$ over a uniformly sampled challenge set $J \subseteq [n]$ with $|J|=c$, the probability that $\Verify$ outputs $accept$ despite the corruption is bounded by:
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{accept}} \leq \frac{\binom{n-d}{c}}{\binom{n}{c}} + \frac{m-1}{p} + \text{negl}(\lambda) \approx (1-\rho)^c + \text{negl}(\lambda).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $E_{\text{miss}}$ be the event that the challenge set $J$ contains no corrupted chunks. Since $J$ is sampled uniformly without replacement from $[n]$, the probability of this event is exactly the hypergeometric ratio:
\[
\Pr[E_{\text{miss}}] = \frac{\binom{n-d}{c}}{\binom{n}{c}}.
\]
When $n \gg c$, this simplifies to $(1-\rho)^c$. In this scenario, the prover can trivially satisfy all verification checks using only intact data.

Let $E_{\text{forge}}$ be the complementary event where $J$ intersects at least one corrupted chunk. For $\Verify$ to still accept, the adversary must successfully forge Merkle authentication paths, satisfy the quotient-consistency pairing, and avoid detection by the Fiat--Shamir folded check. By Theorem~\ref{thm:soundness}, under the $q$-SDH assumption and random oracle model, the probability of bypassing these cryptographic constraints is bounded by $\frac{m-1}{p} + \text{negl}(\lambda)$.

Since $E_{\text{miss}}$ and $E_{\text{forge}}$ are mutually exclusive, the total false-acceptance probability satisfies $P_{\text{accept}} = \Pr[E_{\text{miss}}] + \Pr[E_{\text{forge}}]$. Substituting the respective bounds yields the stated inequality. For standard cryptographic parameters ($p \approx 2^{256}$ and $m \ll p$), the term $\frac{m-1}{p}$ is negligible, and the audit security is strictly governed by the sampling parameter $c$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Parameter Selection]
To achieve a target detection rate $1-\epsilon$ against corruption ratio $\rho$, the challenge size should satisfy $c \geq \frac{\ln \epsilon}{\ln(1-\rho)}$. For example, guaranteeing $99.9\%$ detection for $\rho \geq 1\%$ requires $c \approx 690$. This bound is independent of the batch size $m$ and applies uniformly across all files in the audit.
\end{remark}
